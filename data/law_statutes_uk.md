# UK Magna Carta 1297 cl. 1
Tag: church liberties, establishment

We have granted to God, and by this our present Charter have confirmed for Us and Our Heirs for ever, That the Church of England shall be free, and have all her whole Rights and Liberties inviolable.

# UK Magna Carta 1297 cl. 29
Tag: due process, liberty

No Freeman shall be taken or imprisoned, or be disseised of his Freehold, or Liberties, or free Customs, or be outlawed or exiled, or any other ways destroyed; nor will We not pass upon him, nor condemn him, but by lawful Judgment of his Peers, or by the Law of the Land. We will sell to no man, we will not deny or defer to any man either Justice or Right.

# UK Petition of Right 1628
Tag: taxation, parliament, due process

That no Man hereafter be compelled to make or yield any Gift, Loan, Benevolence, Tax, or such like Charge, without common Consent by Act of Parliament; and that no Freeman shall be taken or imprisoned or be detained, or have his Freehold or Liberties or free Customs taken from him, or be outlawed or exiled, otherwise than by the lawful Judgment of his Peers, or by the Law of the Land; and that no Commission or Authority charge any Man to execute Martial Law in time of Peace, nor Soldiers and Mariners be billetted on the Subject against his Will.

# UK Habeas Corpus Act 1679
Tag: liberty, bail, imprisonment

That upon request of any Person committed, restrained, or imprisoned, a Writ of Habeas Corpus shall be granted; that the Judge, within Twenty Days after delivery of the Writ, shall examine into the true Cause of the Commitment and bail or discharge the Prisoner, or else the same is to stand no longer; that every Judge who shall neglect or refuse to allow any Writ of Habeas Corpus after it is prayed for shall incurr and forfeit the Summe of Five Hundred Pounds; and that no Subject of this Realme shall be sent Prisoner into Scotland, Ireland, Jersey, Guernsey, or to any Place beyond the Seas.

# UK Act of Settlement 1701 s. 1
Tag: succession, protestant

That the most Excellent Princess Sophia, Electress and Duchess Dowager of Hanover, Daughter of the most Excellent Princess Elizabeth, late Queen of Bohemia, Daughter of our late Sovereign Lord King James the First, is hereby declared to be the next in Succession in the Protestant Line to the Imperiall Crown and Dignity of the forsaid Realms, and that the Crown and regal Government of the said Kingdoms and Dominions shall remain and continue to the said most Excellent Princess Sophia and the Heirs of her Body being Protestants.

# UK Act of Settlement 1701 s. 2
Tag: succession, crown

That whosoever shall hereafter come to the Possession of this Crown shall joyn in Communion with the Church of England as by Law established; and that every Person who shall hereafter come to the Possession of this Crown shall profess the Protestant Reformed Religion as established by Law, and if any Person shall afterwards profess the Popish Religion or marry a Papist, they shall for ever be uncapable to inherit, possess or enjoy the Crown.

# UK Act of Settlement 1701 s. 3
Tag: judicial independence

That after the said Limitation shall take Effect as aforesaid, Judges Commissions be made Quamdiu se bene gesserint, and their Salaries ascertained and established; but upon the Address of both Houses of Parliament it may be lawfull to remove them.

# UK Act of Settlement 1701 s. 4
Tag: impeachment, crown

That no Pardon under the Great Seal of England be pleadable to an Impeachment by the Commons in Parliament.

# UK Act of Union 1707 art. II
Tag: succession, union, church

That the Succession to the Monarchy of the United Kingdom of Great Britain shall be, remain, and continue to the most Excellent Princess Sophia and the Heirs of her Body being Protestants, and that the whole Succession shall rest and remain in the same unalterably; and that the Doctrine, Worship, Discipline, and Government of the Church of England and of the Church of Scotland shall remain and continue unalterably established.

# UK Parliament Act 1911 s. 1
Tag: legislation, lords

If a Money Bill, having been passed by the House of Commons and sent up to the House of Lords at least one month before the end of the session, is not passed by the House of Lords without amendment within one month after it is so sent up, the Bill shall, unless the House of Commons direct to the contrary, be presented to His Majesty and become an Act of Parliament on the Royal Assent being signified, notwithstanding that the House of Lords have not consented to the Bill.

# UK Parliament Act 1911 s. 2 (as amended by the Parliament Act 1949)
Tag: legislation, lords

If any Public Bill (other than a Money Bill) is passed by the House of Commons in two successive sessions and, having been sent up to the House of Lords at least one month before the end of the session, is rejected by the House of Lords in each of those sessions, that Bill shall, unless the House of Commons direct to the contrary, be presented to His Majesty and become an Act of Parliament on the Royal Assent being signified, notwithstanding that the House of Lords have not consented to the Bill.

# UK Representation of the People Act 1918 s. 1
Tag: franchise, elections

Every man attaining the age of twenty-one years and not subject to any legal incapacity to vote, who has had the requisite residence qualification, business or occupation qualification, or qualification as a member or graduate of a university, shall be entitled to be registered as a parliamentary elector; and the existing property qualification for men, with all University and similar franchises of voters, is abolished and one uniform franchise is established.

# UK Representation of the People Act 1918 s. 4
Tag: franchise, women

A woman shall be entitled to be registered as a parliamentary elector if she has attained the age of thirty years and is entitled to be registered as a local government elector in respect of property or the occupation of premises, or is the wife of a man entitled to be so registered, or possesses a university qualification; provided that she is a British subject or the wife of a British subject.

# UK Representation of the People Act 1928 s. 1
Tag: franchise, equality

A woman shall be entitled to be registered as a parliamentary elector for a constituency if she has attained the age of twenty-one years, on the same terms as a man, and the qualification of women for the parliamentary franchise shall be the same as the qualification of men.

# UK Human Rights Act 1998 s. 3
Tag: interpretation, convention rights

So far as it is possible to do so, primary legislation and subordinate legislation must be read and given effect in a way which is compatible with the Convention rights.

# UK Human Rights Act 1998 s. 4
Tag: incompatibility, courts

If a court is satisfied that a provision of primary legislation is incompatible with a Convention right, it may make a declaration of that incompatibility; such a declaration does not affect the validity, continuing operation or enforcement of the provision, and is not binding on the parties to the proceedings.

# UK Human Rights Act 1998 s. 6
Tag: public authority, convention rights

It is unlawful for a public authority to act in a way which is incompatible with a Convention right, unless as the result of a provision of primary legislation the authority could not have acted differently.

# UK Human Rights Act 1998 Sch. 1 art. 6
Tag: fair trial

In the determination of his civil rights and obligations or of any criminal charge against him, everyone is entitled to a fair and public hearing within a reasonable time by an independent and impartial tribunal established by law.

# UK Human Rights Act 1998 Sch. 1 art. 8
Tag: privacy, family

Everyone has the right to respect for his private and family life, his home and his correspondence; there shall be no interference by a public authority with the exercise of this right except such as is in accordance with the law and is necessary in a democratic society in the interests of national security, public safety or the economic well-being of the country, for the prevention of disorder or crime, for the protection of health or morals, or for the protection of the rights and freedoms of others.

# UK Human Rights Act 1998 Sch. 1 art. 9
Tag: religion, belief

Everyone has the right to freedom of thought, conscience and religion; this right includes freedom to change his religion or belief and freedom, either alone or in community with others and in public or private, to manifest his religion or belief, in worship, teaching, practice and observance.

# UK Human Rights Act 1998 Sch. 1 art. 10
Tag: expression, freedom

Everyone has the right to freedom of expression. This right shall include freedom to hold opinions and to receive and impart information and ideas without interference by public authority and regardless of frontiers.

# UK Human Rights Act 1998 Sch. 1 art. 11
Tag: assembly, association

Everyone has the right to freedom of peaceful assembly and to freedom of association with others, including the right to form and to join trade unions for the protection of his interests.

# UK Human Rights Act 1998 Sch. 1 art. 14
Tag: non-discrimination

The enjoyment of the rights and freedoms set forth in the Convention shall be secured without discrimination on any ground such as sex, race, colour, language, religion, political or other opinion, national or social origin, association with a national minority, property, birth or other status.

# UK Constitutional Reform Act 2005 s. 3
Tag: judicial independence

The Lord Chancellor, other Ministers of the Crown and all with responsibility for matters relating to the judiciary or otherwise to the administration of justice must uphold the continued independence of the judiciary.
